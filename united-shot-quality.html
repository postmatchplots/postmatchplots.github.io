Why don't United's 203 shots add up to more?

Because so many of them are bad shots. United take more shots than anyone, but the average one is worth less than any other team's, and nearly half come from outside the box, where goals almost never happen.

More shots than anyone, worth less than anyone's

Nobody has shot more than United. But divide their expected goals by their shots and they come out bottom: the average United shot is worth 0.084 xG, against a league average of 0.105.

Man City take 38 fewer shots and create five more expected goals. That's the gap between working the ball into the box and having a dig because nothing else is on.

Non-penalty xG divided by total shots, all 20 PL teams. Hover a dot for the team.

The reason: nearly half come from outside the box

United have taken 93 shots from outside the area. Nobody else has more than 65. That's 46% of all their shots, the highest share in the league, where the average is 34%. City sit at 22%.

Three of those 93 have gone in, 3.2% against a league rate of 4.5%. That's roughly what the chances were worth (3.3 xG), so United aren't unlucky from distance. They're just shooting from places where goals rarely happen.

Bruno Fernandes has had 21 shots from outside the box. None have gone in.

The answer

United's shot count is mostly noise. Strip out the long-range efforts and their chance creation is mid-table: 9th for non-penalty xG from inside the box, 10th for big chances. A team that settles for 25-yard shots this often is usually struggling to get the ball into the box, and that's the real problem in attack.

How this was made

Data from FotMob (Opta), Premier League 2026/27 up to 24 November 2026. Shot locations and xG per shot come from the shot maps of all 110 league matches so far, with own goals left out. Team totals come from FotMob's league leaderboards. Eleven games is a small sample, so treat every number here as a direction, not a verdict.
