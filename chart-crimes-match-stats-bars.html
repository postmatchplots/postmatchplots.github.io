Chart crimes #1  /  Match stats bars  /  3 December 2026

Do the match stats bars tell you who played better?

Not really. After United 4-4 Villa, the usual bars handed United all ten rows, fouls included. What they hid was the story of the game: it was wide open for both sides, and Villa got most of their chances from set pieces.

The crime

You've seen these after every game: two bars facing off on each line, and whoever's bar is longer "wins" the row. Here's United 4-4 Villa on 8 November, drawn the usual way.

Read it cold and you'd think United battered them. Twenty-six shots to 12. Corners 8-1. Forty-eight touches in the box to 18. United win every row on the board.

The game finished 4-4.

Exhibit A

Recreated in the style most apps and broadcasters use. Numbers from FotMob.

What's wrong with it

Everything looks like a tug of war. Possession really is shared: if United have 56%, Villa have 44%. Shots aren't. Villa taking 12 doesn't stop United taking 26. Splitting every row into shares makes every stat look like a contest with a winner.

Every row looks equally important. Corners get the same space as expected goals. United "won" the corners 8-1, which decided very little.

More isn't always better. United win the fouls row too, 18-9. Nobody's celebrating that.

There's no sense of normal. Is 12 shots a lot? What about six big chances? The bars can't tell you, because each team is only measured against the other.

Redraw 1: against a normal game

Put each number next to what a Premier League team usually manages in a match, and a different picture shows up. Both teams made about twice the normal number of big chances and expected goals. Villa were below average for shots, box touches and corners, and still created nearly double the usual xG.

This wasn't United pinning Villa back. It was a wide-open game where both sides kept getting good chances, which is roughly what a 4-4 should look like.

Redraw 2: where the chances came from

This is the bit the bars left out completely, and it's what decided the result. Most of United's xG came from open play: 2.36 of their 3.55. Villa barely got near United's goal from open play, 0.38 xG all game. But they made 1.71 from set pieces, and that's where two of their four goals came from. Another was a penalty, and the last was a 0.04 chance in the first minute.

United won the football. Villa won the set pieces. If that sounds familiar, it's the same problem as the rest of the season.

The answer

No. The usual bars told you United won ten rows to nil, and the game finished 4-4. Redrawn, the story is plain: United ran the open play, Villa lived off set pieces, and both sides created far more than a normal match. If you only look at one thing after a game, make it the chances, ideally the xG race.

How to fix the bars

Only split a bar when the stat really is shared. Possession, yes. Shots, no.

Show what normal looks like. A number means more next to an average.

Put what decides games first. Chances before corners.

Don't let "more" mean "better". Fouls, cards and offsides belong in a different group.
