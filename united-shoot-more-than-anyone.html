United shoot more than anyone.
So why are they 19th?

203 shots, more than any other team. 8 points, fewer than all but one. The gap between those two numbers is the whole story, and most of it is about where the shots come from, and where United let the other lot shoot from.

Lots of shots, not many good ones

On the surface United's attack looks fine. They're 5th for expected goals, and nobody has shot more. But divide one by the other and it falls apart: the average United shot is worth less than any other team's.

Non-penalty xG divided by total shots, all 20 PL teams. Hover a dot for the team.

Man City take 38 fewer shots and create five more expected goals. That's the difference between working the ball into the box and having a dig because nothing else is on.

Nearly half of them come from outside the box

United have taken 93 shots from outside the area. Nobody else has more than 65. That's 46% of all their shots, the highest share in the league, where the average is 34%. City sit at 22%.

Three of those 93 have gone in. That's 3.2%, against a league rate of 4.5%, and it's about what the chances were worth (3.3 xG). So they're not unlucky from distance. They're just shooting from places where goals don't really happen.

Bruno Fernandes has had 21 shots from outside the box this season. None have gone in.

At the other end, the opposite problem

Here's the twist. United don't concede many shots: 146, bang on the league average. But the shots they do concede are the best quality anyone in the league gives away, 0.153 xG each. Opponents barely bother shooting from distance against them (4th fewest long shots faced) because they don't need to. They're getting into the box and getting clean looks.

So United are doing the reverse of what works. They take bad shots and give up good ones.

Only Crystal Palace, who are bottom, have conceded more expected goals. A tempting theory is that United are getting caught on the counter. The data doesn't really back that up: just 2.9 xG has come from fast breaks against them. Most of the damage is in normal open play, which points more to how they defend the box than to how they defend transitions.

Set pieces: a six-goal hole

United have scored one set-piece goal and conceded seven. That's the worst set-piece record in the league by a distance; the next worst teams are at -3. Six goals is two-thirds of their entire -9 goal difference.

Their own corners aren't working either: 39 shots from corners, worth 2.0 xG between them, one goal. The aerial numbers explain some of it. Harry Maguire wins 73% of his aerial duels, top 10% for a centre-back. After him it falls off a cliff: Lisandro Martínez is in the bottom 10% of centre-backs for headers won, and Bryan Mbeumo, Matheus Cunha and Bruno Fernandes are all in the bottom 12% of their position groups.

And Bruno?

He does look different this season, and the numbers agree, mostly. Last season he created about one big chance a game; this season it's under half that. His chances created are down 30% and his xA is down 36%. He's shooting more instead (2.5 to 3.5 a game), and most of the extra shots are the long ones from earlier. He's also doing a lot less without the ball.

One caveat before anyone writes him off. Last season's 21 assists came from 12.3 xA, which is teammates scoring far more of his chances than you'd ever expect. That wasn't going to happen twice. At last season's creation rate he'd have about 3.5 assists by now rather than 2, so the drop is real, just not as dramatic as the headline numbers make it look. Whether it's a change of role, with Cunha and Mbeumo taking more of the creative load, or simply that he's 32, is something the data can't tell you.

What it adds up to

An xG difference of -4 says United have played like a lower mid-table side, not a relegation one. The finishing should come round a bit; they're about four goals behind their chances, spread thinly across lots of players. That's worth a few points over a season.

The rest won't fix itself. Settling for shots from 25 yards, letting opponents walk into the box, and losing the set-piece battle 7-1 are habits, not bad luck. United aren't as bad as 19th. They're nowhere near as unlucky as they'd like to think, either.

How this was made
