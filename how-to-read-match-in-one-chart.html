How to read a Match in one chart

Every Monday, United's latest league game gets the same three charts. The xG race shows who made the better chances and when. The shot map shows where they came from. The star performer shows how United's best-rated player did against his own normal. Here's how to read each one, using the 4-4 with Aston Villa on 8 November.

1. The xG race: who made the better chances, and when

Each line starts at zero and steps up every time that team shoots. The height of each step is the shot's xG, so a tall step is a big chance and a tiny one is a hopeful effort. Dots are goals. Wherever a line ends is the total xG for the match.

Things to look for: which line finishes higher, flat stretches where nobody's creating anything, and goals that came from tiny steps (a worldie, or a deflection) or big steps that didn't produce a dot (a sitter missed).

Here, Villa were 2-0 up inside four minutes, and the two goals were worth 0.92 xG between them, nearly all of it Nicolas Jackson's 0.88 chance. United then built steadily all game. Mason Mount's 0.52 chance hit the post in the 89th minute; Zirkzee and Dorgu scored in stoppage time.

Each step is a shot, the height of the step its xG. Dots are goals. Hover for the shot.

2. The shot map: where the chances came from

Every shot from the match, on one pitch. United always shoot to the right, the opposition to the left. The bigger the circle, the better the chance; filled circles went in.

Look at the penalty boxes. Lots of small circles outside the box means a team was having a go from distance because nothing better was on. Big circles near the goal mean they were getting in behind. United had 26 shots to Villa's 12, but 12 of United's came from outside the box; Villa took 10 of their 12 inside it.

United shoot right, Villa left. Bigger circle, better chance. Filled circles are goals. Own goals left out.

3. The star performer: a good day for him?

The player picked is United's highest-rated outfielder on FotMob who played at least an hour. The chart compares what he did in this match with his own average per 90 minutes this season, so it answers "was this a good day for him?" rather than "is he better than other players?" (that's what percentiles are for).

The white marker in the middle of each bar is his average. A red bar past it means he did more than usual; a grey bar short of it means less. The bar stops at double his average, and an arrow means he went beyond that. If he came off early, his average is scaled down to the minutes he played. If he hasn't played much this season (under 270 minutes), the average for his position is used instead and the caption says so.

Bruno Fernandes against Villa: a penalty, six shots against his usual 3.5, four chances created against 2.8. And one recovery against his usual 3.9. Above his average on 8 of 12.

About the FotMob rating

FotMob gives every player a rating out of 10 after each match, worked out by its own formula from dozens of stats: shots, passes, tackles, duels, mistakes and so on. It's used here for one thing only: picking who to feature. It isn't shown as a verdict, and if the rating picks someone odd (it happens), a different player can be chosen instead.

What one match can't tell you

A single game is a tiny sample. One penalty can make up a quarter of a team's xG, and a player's numbers swing a lot from week to week. Read the xG race as "how good were the chances", not "who deserved to win", and the star performer as one good (or odd) afternoon, not a trend. The trends get their own posts.
