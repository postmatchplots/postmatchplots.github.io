Mainoo  /  30 November 2026

Is Kobbie Mainoo the one thing going right?

Pretty much, yes. Of the 47 central midfielders who've played regularly in the Premier League this season, he's the most accurate passer and the best dribbler, and only one wins the ball back more often. When it's 11 against 11, United are clearly better with him on the pitch. The catch: he almost never shoots or sets up a big chance.

Top of the class with the ball and without it

Line him up against every Premier League central midfielder with 450 minutes or more and the top of his profile is ridiculous. He completes 93% of his passes, more than anyone in the group, and does it at volume: 64 completed passes a game, fifth highest. He's top for dribbles too, beating his man 1.8 times a game and succeeding 77% of the time.

Then he does the other half of the job. Nobody in the group bar Ipswich's Exequiel Palacios wins the ball back more often, and only two make more defensive actions. In a team that's 19th, that's a lot of things going right in one shirt.

Nobody else does both

Plenty of midfielders are good at keeping the ball, and plenty are good at winning it. Put the two side by side and he's almost on his own in the top corner. Only Palacios completes more passes and recovers the ball more often. Declan Rice, Elliot Anderson and Mateus Fernandes pass more; none of them win it back as much.

United are better with him, when it's 11 v 11

Count every minute and it looks like he makes no difference: United's expected goal difference is about the same with him on the pitch as without. But that's because nearly all of United's time with ten men has come with him on. He played 79 minutes at Anfield after Leny Yoro was sent off in the 8th, and 27 at Stamford Bridge after Lisandro Martínez went.

Take out every minute where either side was down to ten and the gap is big. With Mainoo on, United create 2.09 xG per 90 and give away 1.61. Without him, it's 1.53 and 1.90. The actual goals agree: 10-7 with him, 4-9 without.

What he doesn't do

The bottom of his profile is just as clear. He hasn't scored in the league, takes about one shot a game, and hasn't created a single big chance. His xG and xA are both around the middle of the pack at best. He's also in the 4th percentile for sprints, so he's doing all this at walking pace, which is either a worry or the most Mainoo thing imaginable.

He gets the ball to the final third better than almost anyone. What happens next is someone else's problem.

The answer

Yes. He's doing two jobs at the top end of the league, and when he's on the pitch with eleven men, United outplay the opposition on expected goals. Nobody would guess that from the table. The final-third numbers are where he's got room to grow. With 656 minutes and a few hundred without him, the with-and-without split is a strong hint rather than proof, so it's one to check again in the new year.

How this was made

Data from FotMob (Opta), Premier League 2026/27 up to 24 November 2026. Per-90 figures and percentiles come from FotMob's league leaderboards and player pages, comparing him with the 47 central midfielders with 450+ league minutes. The with-and-without split uses the shot maps and substitution times from all 11 United league games, with own goals left out of xG and stoppage time counted as the 90th minute. "11 v 11" removes every minute after a red card for either side: Yoro at Liverpool, Martínez at Chelsea, Foden for City and Bentancur for Spurs. Eleven games is a small sample, so treat every number here as a direction, not a verdict.
