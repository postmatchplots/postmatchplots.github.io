What are percentiles?

A percentile tells you what share of similar players someone is ahead of. 90th percentile means he's better at that thing than 90% of players in his position; 50th is bang average. It's a quick way to read a player's strengths and weaknesses side by side, but it hides how big the gaps actually are.

Line everyone up, see where he stands

Take one stat, say ball recoveries, and line up every comparable player from lowest to highest. A player's percentile is how far along that line he is. Of the 47 Premier League central midfielders compared here, 45 win the ball back less often than Mainoo and one (Ipswich's Exequiel Palacios) does it more.

The sum, for anyone who wants it: the players below him, plus half of those level with him (himself included), divided by the size of the group. That's (45 + 0.5) ÷ 47 = 97%, so 97th percentile. Players on exactly the same number share a percentile.

Ball recoveries per 90 and percentile for the 47 PL central midfielders with 450+ minutes, 2026/27 to 24 November. Hover a dot for the player.

The catch: percentiles squash the gaps

Look at the shape of that chart. In the crowded middle, a tiny difference moves you a long way: going from 3.6 recoveries a game to 4.6 takes you from the 28th percentile to the 49th. At the top, where players are spread out, big differences barely register. Ryan Christie recovers 8.4 a game and James McAtee 7.0, a much bigger gap, and it's worth just four percentiles (95th v 91st).

A percentile tells you the order. It doesn't tell you the distance.

That's why posts here show the raw numbers next to the percentiles, and why a chart like the one above is sometimes better than a percentile bar on its own.

Who he's compared with

A percentile is only as good as the group behind it. On this site that group is always:

Same position. A centre-back in the 10th percentile for shots isn't a problem. Players are grouped as goalkeepers, centre-backs, full-backs, central midfielders, attacking midfielders and wingers, and strikers, using FotMob's positions.

Same league, same season. Premier League only, this season only.

450 minutes or more. That's five full games. Below that, a player who's had one great half an hour can top every chart.

Per 90 minutes. Everything is scaled to a full match, so a player who's played 600 minutes can be compared fairly with one who's played 1,000. It also means a sub who comes on for 20 minutes against tired legs can look better than he is, which the 450-minute rule helps with.

Reading them well

Look at the whole profile. One percentile on its own says little. A midfielder who's 99th for pass accuracy and 18th for big chances created is a safe, tidy passer, not a creator, and both numbers matter.

Low isn't always bad. Some stats depend on the job a player's asked to do. A holding midfielder who rarely shoots might be doing exactly what his manager wants.

Early season, small groups. With a dozen games played, a player can jump 20 percentiles after one good afternoon. Posts say how many games they cover for that reason.

Not every site does it the same way. Other sites use different groups, minimum minutes and methods, so the same player can be 85th on one and 78th on another. Neither is wrong.
