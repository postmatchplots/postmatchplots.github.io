Guide  /  How the numbers work

What are expected points?

Expected points, or xPts, are the points a performance was worth, going by the chances each side made. Play the same match a thousand times with the same shots and they're what you'd average. Add them up over a season and you get a league table of how teams have played, rather than how the ball happened to bounce.

Start with the shots

Take United's first game of the season, away at Hull. United had 21 shots worth 1.83 xG between them; Hull had 8 worth 1.07. Hull scored two from corners and set pieces and won 2-0.

Now treat every shot as its own roll of the dice. Mbeumo's 0.36 chance in the 78th minute goes in 36% of the time; Semi Ajayi's 0.52 chance from a corner goes in about half the time. Work through every combination of shots going in or not and you get the chance of every possible scoreline.

Turn the scorelines into points

Add up every scoreline where United score more and that's their chance of winning: 55%. A draw was 25%, a Hull win 20%. Then score it like the league does: three points for every win, one for every draw.

United: 3 × 0.55 + 0.25 = 1.9 expected points. Hull: 3 × 0.20 + 0.25 = 0.8.

So United played well enough to expect almost two points, and came away with none. Hull's two goals came from chances worth 0.80 xG.

Why not just compare xG?

Because winning on xG by 2.5 to 0.3 and by 1.2 to 1.0 are very different afternoons, and xG alone treats them the same: you "won". Expected points tell you how safe the win should have been. They also deal with the fact that many small chances and one big one aren't the same thing, even if they add up to the same xG: one 0.6 chance goes in 60% of the time, six 0.1 chances give you at least one goal about 47% of the time.

Over a season it adds up

One match is noisy. Eleven start to tell a story. United's chances have been worth 12.9 points, roughly 15th place. They've got 8 and sit 19th. Most of the gap came in three games: Hull, Man City and the 4-4 with Villa.

What expected points can't do

Shots aren't really independent. A team 2-0 up sits back and lets the other side shoot from distance. The model treats every shot as a fresh roll of the dice, so it can't see that.

No shot, no points. Like xG, it only counts chances that ended in a shot.

It's not a refund. "Deserved to win" is a fair reading of a big xPts gap over one game. It isn't a claim on the points. Over a season, though, teams well below their expected points tend to drift back towards them, which is why it's worth watching.
