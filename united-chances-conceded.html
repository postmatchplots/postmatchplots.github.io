Why do United concede so many goals from so few shots?

Because the shots they allow are the best in the league. United face an average number of shots, but each one is worth more than against any other team. Opponents are walking into the box in normal open play, not just catching United on the break.

Not many shots, but very good ones

United have faced 146 shots, bang on the league average. But the shots they allow are worth 0.153 xG each, the highest in the league. Opponents barely bother shooting from distance against them (4th fewest long shots faced) because they don't need to.

Put that next to the attack and United are doing the reverse of what works: they take bad shots and give up good ones. Only Crystal Palace, who are bottom, have conceded more expected goals.

It isn't mainly counter-attacks

The obvious theory is that United push up and get caught on the break. The shot maps don't really back that up. Only 2.9 xG has come from fast breaks against them, and they've conceded once that way.

Most of the damage is in normal open play: 84 shots worth 12.0 xG, and 12 goals. That points more to how United defend their own box than to how they defend transitions. Set pieces are the other leak, which gets its own post.

The answer

United's problem isn't how often teams shoot, it's where from. Opponents are getting clean looks inside the box in ordinary open play. Shots conceded is an average number; chance quality conceded is the worst in the league. That, more than the finishing, is why they're 19th.

How this was made

Team totals come from FotMob's league leaderboards. Eleven games is a small sample, so treat every number here as a direction, not a verdict.
